Exactly. I think we now have the **camera grammar** for the game.

The mistake would be trying to show the entire town at the same level of detail all the time. Instead, the world should **collapse and expand around Bean**.

## Three visual scales

### 1. Town View

The image we just made.

```text
mountains / sky
↓
compressed skyline
↓
a few recognizable high-rises
↓
factories / roads / river / lights
↓
tiny activity
```

You understand the whole settlement at a glance.

We don't need to see every house. A population of several thousand can visually compress into:

* 3–6 prominent towers
* industrial structures
* clusters of smaller buildings
* lights
* traffic
* smoke/steam
* trains/trucks
* distant movement

The **simulation remains detailed even though the graphics are compressed**.

---

# 2. Bean / Building View

This should probably become our primary game view.

Imagine one high-rise taking up most of the phone screen, viewed from a slight angle. SNES dollhouse. Exterior wall partly hides rooms. Bean's floor opens visually.

# 3. Room / Situation View

When something important happens, we push closer.

# Visual resolution follows Bean

TOWN compressed → Bean's block detailed → Bean's building very detailed → Bean's floor fully animated → current situation maximum detail.

Small settlement, mature cyberpunk-era technology, 4,000–8,000 people, two groceries, one clinic, one depot. Cadastral grid underneath; player should almost never see the raw grid.
